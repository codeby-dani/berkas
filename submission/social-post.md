## LinkedIn — full post (using Hook A)

I spent this hackathon building an AI whose main feature is refusing to send my application.

It is called Berkas, which is Indonesian for the file you hand in. You give it a call for
applications, a PDF or just a photo of the page, and it reads it and tells you what it requires. The
deadline, the word caps, the sections you cannot skip.

Then it stops and makes you correct it. A model that reads a document wrong should not also be the
thing that holds you to it, so your correction gets saved next to what the model originally said, and
later anyone can see which of us decided what.

After that it drafts, and then plain Python with no AI anywhere in it decides whether you may submit.
Not "consider shortening this section". Cannot submit.

The first time I ran it end to end I did not answer any of its questions. I just wanted to see if the
pipes connected. It drafted four sections and blocked itself three times, because it would not invent
a host university, a household income, or a project it had no evidence for. It wrote down exactly
what it would need instead.

That was the first real run. I did not stage it.

I'll be honest, I started this wanting to build something that writes better English than me. English
is my second language and I have spent most of this year feeling like I need a script before I can
say anything. But the English was never the thing rejecting me. Somehow the useful version was the
one that reads the rules and then refuses to let me break them.

Built for the All Things Agentic hackathon on Google ADK, Gemini 3.7 Flash and Gemma 4 through
Vertex AI, Cloud Run, Firestore and the Gmail API.

What's one thing you have sent that you wish something had checked first?
